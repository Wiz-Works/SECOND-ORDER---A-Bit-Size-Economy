A randomized policy stance, reshuffled every 50,000 ticks like a new administration — but the SAME head also reviews policy every 5,000 ticks, nudging hawkish/pro-people/mint-bias/gold-aligned in response to real conditions rather than staying frozen all term. Hawkish sets a higher base interest rate. Pro-people scales the landlord rent tax up to 2× or down to 0.5×. Mint bias (0.70-1.30×) leans the money supply independently. The money supply target itself is held PER CAPITA — the head's declared growth rate is growth in coins per head, and the headline target is that times a smoothed population index, so the supply tracks the size of the economy instead of a fixed absolute level. Gold-aligned (0=pro-money, 1=pro-gold) controls BOTH how much welfare gets skimmed into the reserve each cycle (5-30%) and how hard the Fed defends gold's price during a decline — a pro-gold head builds a bigger reserve and spends it more freely defending gold; a pro-money head keeps welfare generous and barely intervenes.

Above 1 = far more standing demand (bids) than supply (asks) right now, so new sellers price higher on purpose. Below 1 = asks piling up with little demand, so new sellers undercut. This is what makes a listing during a shortage cost more than the same listing during a glut.
bid = standing demand: buyers who couldn't get what they needed, waiting at a price. ask = sellers currently listing. When a bid's price meets or beats an ask, they clear automatically — a bid sitting with no matching ask is visible unmet demand, not a dead number. vol = cumulative units traded either way. Gold is now a full market item too, priced in coins like everything else — this is what finally gives it real, liquid price discovery instead of relying on thin barter.

Buying injects real coins into the family (split evenly among members) and issues new shares at the current fair price; selling extracts real coins from members' actual liquid balances, capped at what's there — a land-rich, cash-poor family may not fully pay out. Agents with a high enough retail gene (≥0.55) buy their assigned category — food, gold, iron, oil, glass, or stone/sand — whenever it's trading under its own 5,000-tick moving average, and resell above that same average once holding stock. Both the buy-in discount required and the resale margin scale with prudence (bigger safety cushion) and speculate (holding out for a bigger dip / better spread) — a maxed-out retailer waits for a real ~12% dip before buying and charges up to ~25% on the way back out; a bold, low-prudence one trades on almost any softness at all. Retailers can also build storage on land they own, raising their carry capacity for trade goods from any tile they hold it on. (Target margin above is the intended spread built into each retailer's genes, not realized profit — inventory isn't cost-basis tracked, so actual $ earned per sale isn't measured yet.)

A landlord is anyone who currently owns a house tile in the rental pool — every first-floor house build enters that pool automatically, whether the owner lives there or leases it out. Rent price per tenant is 4×the owner's own rentTake gene, paid per tick, billed upfront for a full lease block. Rent extraction as a share of GDP feeds directly into the rent tax rate, which rises automatically the more rent-dominant the economy becomes.

A drill is a permanent structure on land you own — produces gold and sand indefinitely instead of depleting a fixed deposit, the actual fix for eventual resource exhaustion. Costs 20 iron to build (no oil needed up front) but starts at only 25% efficiency; three upgrades (10i/2o, 20i/4o, 30i/8o) each add another 25%, reaching 100% at full upgrade. Gold goes straight to whoever currently owns the tile; sand tops up that tile's own deposit for the normal glass-smelting pipeline. Runs on its own oil tank refueled from the market. If you sell the land, whoever owns it now gets the output.

An oil drill runs on WORKERS instead of fuel — 20 iron to build, then efficiency scales linearly with headcount, 0% at zero workers up to 100% at 10. Each worker costs the owner real wages every tick (70% of that worker's share of the output's value, at the live oil price — wages track what the labor actually produces) — if the owner can't afford it, that worker is fired immediately. Workers keep living normally (foraging, trading, moving) but burn energy twice as fast the whole time they're employed, and can only hold one drilling job at a time. A cash-strapped owner genuinely cannot sustain a full crew — this isn't free output, it's a real bet.

18. Natural Disasters

No active disasters.
Recent Events
—
Heavy rain / drought / heatwave / cold wave hit one biome every ~50k-100k ticks (occasionally two in an unlucky stretch) and temporarily raise or lower crop growth there. Earthquakes and blizzards are independent, far rarer (~once per 100k ticks), instant, and area-limited within a biome — earthquakes destroy structures, blizzards wipe standing crops (which regrow naturally afterward). Sandstorms are the one GOOD disaster — checked every 200k ticks with a low chance of firing, they replenish 10%-80% of Desert sand deposits, partially resetting the otherwise one-way depletion that natural sand never recovers from on its own.
